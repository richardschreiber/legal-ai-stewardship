# The Four AI Stewardship Practices
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Four AI Stewardship Practices are the minimum viable governance infrastructure for any legal or advocacy organization deploying artificial intelligence. They are four observable disciplines any general counsel or managing partner can audit in an afternoon — and the absence of any one of them constitutes a governance gap.

*"These four practices do not require a chief AI officer. They require a general counsel willing to own the discipline and a board willing to ratify it."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## The Four Practices

### Practice 1 — Written, Board-Ratified AI Use Policy

A written AI use policy that covers every department — not just legal. Must be less than 12 months old to qualify as a current governance document rather than a historical artifact.

**What it covers:**
- Permitted and prohibited uses of AI tools across every department
- Classification of AI outputs by risk tier
- Human review requirements before AI output reaches a regulator, court, counterparty, or client
- Named accountability owner for policy enforcement
- Renewal and review cadence

**What disqualifies it:**
- Written more than 12 months ago without revision
- Covers only the legal department
- Has not been ratified by the board or executive leadership
- Exists as a document but has not been communicated to staff

---

### Practice 2 — Documented AI Inventory

A living inventory of every AI system in use across the organization. Updated quarterly. Board sees it at every meeting.

**What it documents:**
- Every AI system by name — including tools adopted informally by individual staff (shadow AI)
- Risk tier classification for each system
- Data flows — what data each system processes, stores, or transmits
- Vendor names and contractual indemnity status
- Named accountability owner for each system
- Date last reviewed

**What disqualifies it:**
- Not updated in the last quarter
- Does not include shadow AI
- Has named systems but no named accountability owners
- Board has never seen it

---

### Practice 3 — Human-in-the-Loop Standard

A documented standard specifying what human review is required before any AI-generated output touches a regulator, customer, counterparty, or court.

**What it specifies:**
- Which AI outputs require human review before use
- What that review looks like — who conducts it, on what cadence, with what sign-off requirement
- What constitutes adequate verification for AI-generated legal citations
- What constitutes adequate review for AI-generated client communications

**The core principle:**
The human-in-the-loop standard is not a compliance checkbox. It is the operational expression of the conviction that AI is a tool — and humans drive. You cannot outsource the steering wheel.

---

### Practice 4 — Rehearsed Incident Response Playbook

A written incident response playbook covering AI-specific failure scenarios — rehearsed at least once annually.

**Three required scenarios:**
1. An AI hallucination reaches a customer, counterparty, or court
2. A vendor AI system causes a regulatory breach or data exposure
3. An internal AI model produces a discriminatory output

**What rehearsal requires:**
- A tabletop exercise using a real scenario
- Named roles for every step in the response
- Documentation of the rehearsal in board minutes
- A date set for the next rehearsal before the current one concludes

**What disqualifies it:**
- Written but never rehearsed
- Rehearsed more than 12 months ago
- Does not cover AI-specific scenarios — only general data breach scenarios
- Roles are named by title only, not by individual name

## What These Four Practices Replace

The old model: AI governance as a technology procurement question — handled by IT, reviewed by legal, filed and forgotten.

AI Stewardship model: AI governance as a fiduciary discipline — owned by the general counsel or managing partner, ratified by the board, rehearsed annually, and updated quarterly.

*"Adoption is easy. Stewardship is hard."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## Who This Framework Is For

- General counsel at mid-market companies managing AI adoption without a dedicated AI governance team
- Managing partners of small and mid-sized law firms deploying AI in client work
- Legal aid managing attorneys governing AI tools with no dedicated IT staff
- Any organization whose AI-generated outputs touch a regulator, court, or vulnerable population
