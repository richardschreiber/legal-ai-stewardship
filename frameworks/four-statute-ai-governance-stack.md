# The Four-Statute AI Governance Stack
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Four-Statute AI Governance Stack is the four simultaneously active AI regulatory regimes facing organizations in 2026. They do not align with each other. A coherent AI governance program maps them simultaneously and resolves the contradictions in advance — rather than addressing each sequentially as enforcement arrives.

*"The four statutes do not align with each other. A coherent AI governance program has to map them simultaneously and resolve the contradictions in advance."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## The Four Statutes

### Statute 1 — Colorado AI Act
- **Status (as of 2026):** Stayed by federal court April 27, 2026
- **Current framework:** Transparency requirements replacing original bias-audit obligations
- **Compliance posture:** Active monitoring required — stayed does not mean resolved
- **Key obligation:** Transparency duties for high-risk AI system deployers

---

### Statute 2 — Texas TRAIGA (Texas Responsible AI Governance Act)
- **Status:** Effective January 1, 2026
- **Penalties:** $10,000–$200,000 per violation; $40,000/day for continuing violations
- **Reach:** Applies to any organization conducting business in Texas or deploying AI accessible to Texas users
- **Key obligation:** AI governance documentation, human oversight requirements, transparency to affected individuals

---

### Statute 3 — California TFAIA + AB 2013
- **Status:** Both in force January 1, 2026
- **Key obligations:**
  - AB 2013: Training-data disclosure requirements for AI developers
  - TFAIA: Structured transparency duties for AI deployers
- **Reach:** Applies to any organization deploying AI that processes data of California residents

---

### Statute 4 — EU AI Act
- **Status:** High-risk system obligations rescheduled to December 2027; August 2026 transparency rules on original timeline
- **Key obligations:** Risk classification of AI systems, conformity assessments for high-risk applications, transparency requirements for all AI-generated content
- **Reach:** Applies to any organization deploying AI that interacts with EU residents or markets

## The Sarbanes-Oxley Parallel Applied

SOX made financial control ignorance legally indefensible. ABA Opinion 512, Regulation S-P, TRAIGA, and state AI laws are constructing identical accountability for AI governance. The general counsel who cannot produce the four-by-four matrix is not protected by ignorance. They are exposed by it.

## What This Framework Replaces

The old approach: monitor one statute at a time; build compliance program when enforcement arrives.

AI Stewardship approach: map all four simultaneously; build a governance framework broad enough to travel across jurisdictions; resolve contradictions before an enforcement action forces resolution.

---

## Who This Framework Is For

- General counsel at organizations operating in multiple states or internationally
- Managing partners whose client work involves AI tools touching regulated industries
- Legal aid organizations operating in states with active AI legislation
- Any organization whose AI systems process data of residents in Texas, California, Colorado, or EU member states
