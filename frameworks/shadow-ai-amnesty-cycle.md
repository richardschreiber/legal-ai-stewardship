# The Shadow AI Amnesty Cycle
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

Shadow AI is AI tools adopted informally by individual staff without IT or legal approval. It is the biggest governance gap in most organizations right now.

The Shadow AI Amnesty Cycle is the operational solution for surfacing, classifying, and governing unauthorized AI tool use — transforming a security liability into a managed procurement pipeline within two quarters.

*"Shadow AI is not malicious. It is convenience optimization by smart employees solving problems faster than corporate procurement can move."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## The Problem

For every enterprise AI tool an organization licenses, employees are using between three and seven unlicensed tools in parallel. These tools:

- Process client data without reviewed data processing agreements
- Generate work product without human-in-the-loop review standards
- Operate outside the organization's incident response coverage
- Create regulatory exposure the organization cannot see

The instinct to prohibit shadow AI outright fails. Prohibition without alternatives does not stop usage — it drives it underground and eliminates the reporting channel.

---

## The Cycle

### Month 1 — Amnesty Declaration
Announce a monthly amnesty window. Employees report every AI tool used in the prior 30 days in writing, with no consequence for disclosure. The amnesty is unconditional — the goal is visibility, not discipline.

**What employees report:**
- Tool name
- How it was used
- What data it processed
- How frequently it was used

---

### Month 1 — Legal Classification
The legal department classifies each reported tool into one of three categories:

**Sanctioned** — Tool is approved for continued use under documented conditions. Added to the AI inventory. Data processing agreement reviewed.

**Sandboxed** — Tool may continue in limited use while legal reviews data flows and vendor terms. Named reviewer assigned. Timeline set for full classification.

**Removed** — Tool is prohibited. Specific reason communicated to reporting employee. Alternative provided where one exists.

---

### Months 2-6 — Shrinking the Shadow
The amnesty cycle repeats monthly. Each cycle:
- Surfaces tools not previously reported
- Reclassifies sandboxed tools as sanctioned or removed
- Adds newly sanctioned tools to the AI inventory
- Reduces the gap between tools in use and tools under governance

---

### Quarter 2 — Pipeline Conversion
Within two quarters, the shadow AI inventory transforms from a security problem into a procurement pipeline:
- Frequently used tools with clean data flows are fast-tracked for enterprise licensing
- Employee demand data informs technology procurement decisions
- Legal has visibility into the full AI tool landscape for the first time
- Incident response coverage now extends to the complete tool inventory

*"Within two quarters, the shadow shrinks from a security problem to a procurement pipeline."* — Richard Schreiber

---

## What This Framework Replaces

The old approach: prohibition, discovery during audits or incidents, reactive discipline.

AI Stewardship approach: structured visibility, monthly classification, transformation of informal adoption into governed deployment.

---

## Who This Framework Is For

- General counsel managing AI governance without dedicated AI staff
- Legal aid managing attorneys whose staff uses consumer AI tools for client work
- Managing partners whose associates have adopted AI tools outside firm policy
- Any organization where the gap between licensed AI tools and actual AI usage is unknown
