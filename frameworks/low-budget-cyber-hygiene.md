# The Low-Budget Cyber Hygiene Framework
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Low-Budget Cyber Hygiene Framework is the security control set for resource-constrained legal aid organizations — the legal aid society, public defender office, or access-to-justice nonprofit with 5-25 attorneys, no dedicated IT staff, and an annual budget under $5 million. Four controls, implementable this week, at a total cost most organizations can absorb without a board approval cycle.

It exists because the organizations holding the most sensitive client data in the legal system — the files of domestic violence survivors, undocumented immigrants, and families in housing court — are the least resourced to protect it. Governance is not a luxury for well-funded organizations. This framework is the proof.

---

## The Four Controls

### Control 1 — Multi-Factor Authentication (Free)
MFA on every account that touches client data — email, case management, cloud storage. Cost: zero. The single highest-return security action available to any organization, and the one most ransomware entry points still depend on being absent.

### Control 2 — Quarterly Phishing Training
Four short sessions a year, because the attack that opens 1,200 client files does not arrive as code — it arrives as an email a tired staff attorney opens on a Tuesday. Training converts the staff from the attack surface into the detection layer.

### Control 3 — Offsite Encrypted Backups (Under $100/Month)
Encrypted, offsite, tested. The difference between a ransomware note that ends the organization's week and one that ends its existence is whether the data exists somewhere the attacker cannot reach.

### Control 4 — Vendor Security Reviews with Three Written Questions
Every vendor touching client data answers three questions in writing: how the data is encrypted, who can access it, and what happens when they are breached. Written answers create accountability; verbal assurances create discovery exhibits.

## What It Is Not

- It is not enterprise cybersecurity scaled down. It is a different framework built for the budget that actually exists.
- It is not a substitute for a written AI use policy — it is the security floor beneath one. (See: The Four AI Stewardship Practices.)
- It is not optional because the organization is a nonprofit. The clients' stakes make it less optional, not more.
