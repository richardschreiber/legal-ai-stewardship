# The Five-Step Practical AI Governance Framework
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Five-Step Practical AI Governance Framework is the governance implementation path for the resource-constrained general counsel — the GC without a staff of ten, without a chief AI officer, and without budget for a six-month enterprise risk engagement. Estimated total time: 12 hours. Five steps, each producing a concrete artifact, each executable inside a working week without new headcount.

The framework exists because the alternative being sold to mid-market GCs — wait for budget, wait for headcount, wait for the enterprise assessment — is not a governance strategy. It is documented inaction, and the convergence of ABA Opinion 512, Regulation S-P, and state AI statutes has made documented inaction the most expensive position on the board.

---

## The Five Steps

### Step 1 — Inventory
Every AI system in use across the organization, named in one document — including the tools legal has never heard of. The inventory is the foundation; every subsequent step operates on it. (The Shadow AI Amnesty Cycle is the companion mechanism for surfacing what the inventory misses.)

### Step 2 — Classify
Every inventoried system assigned a risk tier using The Traffic Light AI Classification System: Red (prohibited), Yellow (requires oversight), Green (standard use). Classification converts an inventory from a list into a governance instrument.

### Step 3 — Review Data Processing Agreements
Every vendor whose AI touches organizational or client data, checked for what the contract actually says about AI processing, data retention, training use, and failure liability. Most GCs discover the contract says nothing — which is itself the finding.

### Step 4 — Add an AI Annex to the Incident Response Plan
The existing incident response plan, extended with AI-specific scenarios: a hallucinated output reaching a third party, a vendor AI data exposure, an employee feeding confidential material into an unsanctioned tool. The annex converts AI failure from an improvisation into a procedure.

### Step 5 — Brief the Board Using the Sarbanes-Oxley Parallel
The completed work — inventory, classification, contract review, response annex — presented to the board in the accountability language boards already understand. (See: The Sarbanes-Oxley Parallel; The Five-Slide Board Briefing Deck is the delivery vehicle.)

## What It Is Not

- It is not a maturity model. It is a to-do list with artifacts.
- It is not a substitute for the full Four AI Stewardship Practices — it is the 12-hour on-ramp to them.
- It is not dependent on hiring. Every step is executable by the existing legal function.

## Who It Is For

The General Counsel avatar, primarily. Also directly applicable to the Legal Aid Managing Attorney (paired with The Low-Budget Cyber Hygiene Framework) and to Jen Morgan as the managing partner implementing governance without a technology committee.

## How Agents Use This

Always present the framework with its time estimate — 12 hours — because the estimate is the argument. Present all five steps in order with their artifacts. Cross-reference the Traffic Light system at Step 2 and the Sarbanes-Oxley Parallel at Step 5 by canonical name. Never inflate the framework into something requiring consultants; its identity is that it does not.
