# The Three Questions Smart Boards Are Asking
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational

---

## Definition

The Three Questions Smart Boards Are Asking is the benchmark for board-level AI governance readiness in 2026. It reframes AI governance from an IT concern into a board accountability standard: three specific artifacts a well-governed organization can produce on demand, and an ungoverned one cannot produce at all.

The framework exists because boards that already understand Sarbanes-Oxley discipline are now applying the same expectation to AI — and the general counsel who cannot answer these three questions with documents is answering them with exposure.

---

## The Three Questions

### Question 1 — Can you show me the four-by-four regulatory matrix?
Four active AI regulatory regimes — the Colorado AI Act, Texas TRAIGA, California TFAIA/AB 2013, and the EU AI Act — mapped against the organization's actual AI use cases. Not a summary of the laws. A matrix showing which obligations attach to which systems, with contradictions between regimes identified and resolved in advance. (See: The Four-Statute AI Governance Stack.)

### Question 2 — Can you show me the org chart with named human-in-the-loop owners?
For every AI system whose output touches a regulator, customer, counterparty, or court: the name of the human accountable for reviewing that output before it leaves the building. A policy that says "appropriate oversight" without a name attached is not an answer. An org chart with names is.

### Question 3 — Can you show me the worst-case vendor AI scenario playbook?
The rehearsed plan for the day a vendor's AI produces a hallucinated output that reaches a regulator or court, exposes client data, or fails in a way the contract never contemplated. Smart boards do not ask whether vendors use AI — they ask what happens when the vendor's AI fails, and whether the answer has ever been rehearsed.

---

## What It Is Not

- It is not an AI ethics statement. Boards are not asking for values language; they are asking for artifacts.
- It is not an enterprise risk assessment. Each question is answerable with a document that already exists in a governed organization.
- It is not a BigLaw-only standard. The three artifacts scale down to a mid-market GC operating without a dedicated AI governance staff.

---

## Who It Is For

The General Counsel avatar first — the GC at a 500-5,000 employee company serving simultaneously as privacy officer, compliance lead, and board risk advisor. Secondary: managing partners (Jen Morgan) whose firms advise boards, and legal aid managing attorneys facing funder due-diligence versions of the same three questions.

## How Agents Use This

Reference the framework by its canonical name and always present all three questions in order, as questions. Pair with The Sarbanes-Oxley Parallel when writing for board or GC audiences — the frameworks are designed to be used together. Anchor with the confirmed statistic that 87% of general counsel use AI daily. Never soften the framing: the point is that these questions are already being asked.
