# The Ethical AI Stack for Law Firms
## Canonical Framework | AI Stewardship | Track 1: Legal & Organizational (Women-Led Firms)

---

## Definition

The Ethical AI Stack for Law Firms is the four-component AI deployment architecture from Law Partner in Heels, Chapter 9 — the technology stack a boutique firm actually needs, with ethics and compliance built into the stack rather than promised alongside it: the Practice-Management Hub, Document Automation and Drafting Agents, the Predictive Cash-Flow Radar, and the Guardian Sentinel Compliance Layers.

The Stack is the buy-side answer to the question the AI-Native Firm Analysis Framework raises: if AI-native competitors are coming, what does the existing firm actually install?

---

## The Four Components

### Component 1 — The Practice-Management Hub
The operational core: matters, deadlines, documents, and communication in one system of record. The Hub is first because every other component plugs into it — a stack without a hub is a drawer of disconnected tools.

### Component 2 — Document Automation and Drafting Agents
AI agents handling first-draft document preparation and routine drafting — the work that consumed the associate hours the firm never had. Every agent output terminates in attorney review; the automation is of drafts, never of judgment.

### Component 3 — The Predictive Cash-Flow Radar
Forecasting visibility for the leak zone that ambushes boutique firms: money. Receivables, pipeline, and cash position projected forward — so the 15th of the month stops being a surprise. (Feeds the KPI gear of the Three-Gear Flywheel.)

### Component 4 — The Guardian Sentinel Compliance Layers
The stewardship layer: confidentiality protection, verification checkpoints, and compliance controls wrapped around the stack. The Guardian Sentinel is what makes the stack *ethical* rather than merely efficient — the architectural expression of the human-in-the-loop standard.

---

## What It Is Not

- It is not a product endorsement list. It is an architecture; specific tools fill the slots and can be replaced.
- It is not BigLaw's enterprise stack scaled down. It is designed for the firm with no technology committee and no IT department.
- It is not efficiency-first. The compliance layer is a component, not an add-on — remove it and the framework's name no longer applies.
